Course structure

This course combines self-study elements (the chapters on this website) with weekly in-person interactive sessions. The syllabus consists of three main parts, as reflected by the structure of this website:

  1. Lecture notes: the chapters on this website explain the concepts and methods behind marketing analytics and show you how to apply them using the statistical software R.
  2. Individual assignments: the individual assignments require you to apply the acquired knowledge to new data sets.
  3. Group project: in the group project you will design and conduct your own market research project — including a real randomized experiment — and transfer the knowledge to a real business setting.

The general approach is that students first familiarize themselves with the contents by going through the materials on their own. This self-study process is complemented with in-person weekly interactive sessions, which provide ample opportunities to ask questions and clarify points that require further discussion. Before the second session, you should complete the self-paced R onboarding module, which walks you through installing R and RStudio (or working in Posit Cloud as a zero-install alternative).

Group project

The marketing research group project consists of multiple consecutive steps:

  • the design of a questionnaire containing a randomized experiment
  • data collection using an online survey
  • data handling & data analysis
  • presentation of your results

We will offer coaching sessions throughout this process, providing feedback and allowing you to ask questions. The coaching slots and submission dates will be announced here (TBC); the in-class coaching sessions are marked in the lecture schedule above.

Your main point of contact for the group project is me (nils.wloemert@wu.ac.at).

Course materials

Main reference

The main reference for this course is this website along with the corresponding slides (linked from each chapter). The aim of the materials is to condense the contents and direct your attention to the most relevant aspects, so that we can focus our attention during the classroom sessions on application, discussion, and the points that require clarification.

At the end of each chapter, you will find a section with references. It is highly recommended that you consult these references for further clarification in case you require additional information on a topic.

Further readings

In addition to these lecture notes, there are many excellent books available (many of them for free) that focus on different aspects of R and data analysis. There are so many free resources available by now that a team of R programmers has set up a website that provides an overview of the available resources by topic: Big Book of R.

In case you would like to learn more about the topics of this course, I can particularly recommend the following books:

  • Data Analysis for Business, Economics, and Policy (Békés & Kézdi) — an excellent, case-based treatment of data analysis, prediction, and causal inference whose spirit this course shares.
  • R for Data Science — Hadley Wickham’s introduction to R as a tool for doing data science, focusing on the tidyverse. [FREE online version]
  • An Introduction to Statistical Learning — an introduction to statistical learning methods, from linear regression to more advanced methods. [FREE online version]
  • R for Marketing Research and Analytics — designed to teach R to marketing practitioners and data scientists.
  • Statistical Inference via Data Science — an accessible treatment of statistical inference. [FREE online version]
  • Text Mining with R — analyzing unstructured data (texts) using R. [FREE online version]
  • R Graphics Cookbook — more than 150 recipes for generating high-quality graphs quickly. [FREE online version]
  • Using R For Introductory Econometrics — an introduction to R with a focus on standard tools and methods used in econometrics. [FREE online version]
  • Discovering Statistics Using R (Field, Miles & Field, 2012) — an accessible and comprehensive introduction to statistics.
